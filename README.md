# Asean-economies-
Analyze and compare 9 ASEAN countries' economies using Python and SQL, and display them using a dashboard. 

# Highlights of the Economic Dashboard
Summary of macroeconomic indicators, growth drivers, classifications, and structural insights.

## Economic Performance as of 2023
Highest GDP: China has the largest GDP, at around 36,381.1 billion USD PPP  
Highest GDP Growth: Turkey with 5.11%  
Highest GDP Growth over the past 10 years: China has grown 5.91% per year  
Highest GDP per capita: Singapore with $143,287 per capita PPP  
Highest Productivity (GDP per hour PPP): Singapore  
Highest Population: India  

## Sources of GDP Growth
Manufacturing: 9 out of 9 countries have manufacturing growth  
Exchange Rate: 9 out of 9 countries' currencies have been devalued relative to 1970.  
Finance Sector: 9 out of 9 countries have an expanding finance sector  
Demographics: Only high-income countries have a declining proportion of the working population. Most developing and middle-income countries have a growing working-age population.  
Inflation: Japan has deflation while Turkey has hyperinflation. The rest have a stable rate of inflation.  
Trade: Most all are export-oriented economies. The outlier is Singapore, which has exports of over 200% of GDP only due to its entrepôt status.  

## Developing, Middle-Income, and Advanced Economies
Developing: India, Thailand, and Vietnam  
Middle-Income: China, Turkey, and Malaysia  
High-Income: Korea, Japan, and Singapore  

## Key Insights
- Many Asian countries rely on higher manufacturing output for growth.  
- Countries that have high growth also tend to have a higher working-age population, as more people earning income and paying taxes increase GDP.  
- Most countries are export-oriented, meaning they rely on exports for growth.  
- Most countries devalue their currency to make their exports cheaper.  
- How a country transforms into an advanced economy is through continued growth in manufacturing, gross fixed capital formation, a ballooning finance sector, in addition to a growing working-age population.  
- Developed countries tend to have a lower growth rate than developing/middle-income countries due to the catch-up effect.   
- The proportion of the working-age population tends to decline due to an aging population. Countries that have high growth also tend to have a higher working-age population, as more people earning income and paying taxes increase GDP.
